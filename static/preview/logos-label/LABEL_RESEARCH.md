**Spec used for the Spectre Brands centre label:** **78 × 46 mm paper with r ≈ 2.4 mm corners**, centred in the recess at x 54.5, y 24.4. That leaves about 1 mm of recess showing on each side, as on real tapes.

Placement notes from the photos:
- Rental stores usually put their big name or "rewind" sticker on the **front flap edge or the rear spine**, not on the face. The Blockbuster photo shows a full-width "Please remember to rewind!" strip along the flap side.
- Barcodes went "on the face or sides of the tape, never on the tape gate" (r/VHS thread, Feb 2026).
- Our crooked orange strip over the rear grip is a stylisation of those stickers.

## 2. Typical layouts

### (a) Blank-tape maker labels (TDK, Fuji, Maxell, Sony, Memorex, Scotch, Sanyo, BASF)
- **Maker band plus grade:**
  - A coloured band or end block carries the maker name and grade name (HS, EHG, HGX, Super AG, EG+).
  - The length code is the biggest type on the label: **T-120** for NTSC and **E-180** for PAL.
- **Recording-time scale:**
  - A printed table, "Recording Time in minutes": SP 120 / LP 240 / EP 360 (Scotch E240 shows SP 120/180/210/240 over LP 240/360/420/480).
  - It often has small marker boxes for ticking the speed used. On a T-120, SP = 2 h, LP = 4 h and EP = 6 h (Stanford tape guide).
- **Write-in area:** 3–4 ruled lines left blank for the title, sometimes with DATE, STEREO/MONO and noise-reduction ON/OFF checkboxes (Sanyo).
- **Other common features:**
  - Pictogram tick-boxes for genre (BASF: teddy bear, globe, UFO, masks, music note, film star).
  - A "THIS SIDE IN" arrow.
  - Decorative graduated stripe bands (Scotch).
  - Format and compliance marks, which we must avoid.
- **Reference photos:** VHS tape with time scale (Scotch); Sanyo VHS tape made by Funai; Video 1 (BASF label, hand-filled); Tdk video 001; Maxell VHS tapes 01; Frontside of a VHS Tape (Maxell, hand-written).
- Collector catalogues: fallinsideahole.com (Maxell/Fuji tapes with runtime charts and checkmark boxes), vhsmester.com/maxell (grade names by year).

### (b) Video-store rental labels and stickers
- **Store identity:** a name or logo sticker, often "Property of …" with the store address or phone.
- **Inventory:** a barcode or inventory-number sticker, usually Code 39 style with a human-readable number.
- **Shelving cues:** genre flags (HORROR, the "green circle" for horror at some chains), NEW RELEASE, OVERNIGHT, and price stickers ("£1 OVERNIGHT", "99¢").
- **"Be Kind, Rewind"** stickers in endless variants, often with a smiley. Stores fined customers for unrewound tapes.
- **Layering:** later stickers go over older ones (re-pricing, "CLOSING DOWN").
- **Studio rental-only cassettes:** these carried "property of … rented … only for the rental period specified" text.
- **Sources:**
  - "Be Kind, Rewind: The Lost Art of VHS Labels" (that80sdude.com);
  - *Stuck on VHS: A Visual History of Video Store Stickers* (Josh Schafer, Birth.Movies.Death; covered in the Austin Chronicle and Irish Film Critic);
  - Joshua Hedges' *Video Store Relics* zine (Lunchmeat);
  - "Adhesive Archaeology" (Plutonic Rainbows, Apr 2026);
  - Warner Home Video rental-warning text (avid.wiki);
  - Commons photo "Blockbuster VHS Tape" (structure only).

### (c) Studio release labels
- **Paper and layout:** white or cream label, black or one-colour print, roughly symmetrical.
- **Distributor mark:** top-left, often in a box (e.g. "AVID Home Entertainment").
- **Title:** large, centred.
- **Running-time and catalogue line:** e.g. "APPROX. 90 MINS. / CAT. NO. 51019" or "Running time: 78 minutes".
- **Small print:** a 2–4 line WARNING paragraph about unauthorised copying, then a © line and a catalogue code (e.g. "VWA1103").
- **Reference photos:**
  - Dudes (1991 AVID label), CC BY-SA 4.0;
  - Begotten (1995 World Artists label), public domain;
  - Nero Wolfe production labels, public domain (typed title, version and duration only).

## 3. What we took from this
- **Size and position:** 78 × 46 mm, centred in the recess.
- **Three structures to borrow:** the blank-tape scale (speed table to DEAD/GHOST table, T-length to T-38), the studio layout (distributor box, big title, CAT. NO., small-print notice) and rental add-ons (barcode, genre flags, rewind reminder, layered stickers).
- **Left out (marks and trade dress):**
  - the VHS logo (JVC);
  - Dolby and Hi-Fi marks;
  - the FBI seal;
  - real maker, studio and store names or logos;
  - Blockbuster's blue and yellow;
  - the "rainbow ball" style of the Scotch label.

## 4. How these labels wear (for the "used" textured versions)
What the photos show:
- **Frontside of a VHS Tape (Maxell):** the paper label has gone grey-beige, with scuffed edges and ballpoint handwriting.
- **Video 1 (BASF):** two marker colours on one label, a small sticker applied over the label, and edge lifting.
- **Blockbuster VHS Tape:** a marker scrawl ("PIT") over the printed rewind sticker, and an off-white, grimy edge.
- **Old videocassette and Tdk video 001:** faded inks.

What collectors describe:
- **Layered stickers:** re-pricing, genre flags and "CLOSING DOWN" over older stickers. Peeling a corner reveals another sticker underneath (Plutonic Rainbows, "Adhesive Archaeology", 2026).
- **"Be Kind, Rewind" stickers** in endless variants, and genre and price flags curling at the corners (that80sdude; *Stuck on VHS*).
- **"Property of" markings and rental stickers** are common on ex-rental tapes, and purists count them against value (Relic collector guide).

General paper and ink ageing:
- Labels yellow and darken at the edges.
- Red and orange dyes fade before black.
- Adhesive bleeds through as translucent spots and leaves grey residue when a sticker is removed.

The wear spec we applied is in `WEAR.md`.
