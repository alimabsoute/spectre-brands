# Blockbuster in numbers

Blockbuster ended 2004 with 9,094 stores in 25 countries and just over $6 billion in revenue.[^1] That year it borrowed heavily to pay a special distribution of $5.00 a share, about $905.6M, as it left its parent, Viacom.[^2] Long-term debt rose from $75.1M to $1,119.7M in twelve months.[^1]

A leveraged buyout is a common way to buy a chain, and the company's debt looked like one even though nobody had arranged it. Operating leverage then worked against it. Rent and payroll stayed put while revenue fell. Late fees mattered more than the stores did. Extended viewing fees were $692.6M in 1999, or 15.5% of revenue.[^3]

Marc Randolph, Netflix's co-founder, writes that John Antioco "was struggling not to laugh" when Netflix named a price of $50M in September 2000.[^4] Blockbuster's own filings do not mention the meeting. The account is Randolph's.

The chart is printed in landscape orientation so the 2004 peak fits on one page, and the regression in the appendix uses robust standard errors. Orbitz, the clear fruit drink with gel balls, was made by Clearly Canadian, which stopped making it by 1999. Webvan kept its headquarters in Foster City, California, and nothing about that is a metaphor.

Dish Network bought substantially all of the assets in April 2011 for $320.6M.[^5] One franchised store in Bend, Oregon, still rents films under the name, and it has been the only one in the world since 2019.
